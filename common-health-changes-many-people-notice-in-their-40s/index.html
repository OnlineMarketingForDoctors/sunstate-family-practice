Common Health Changes Many People Notice in Their 40s

Did you know the human body changes considerably during your 40s? That’s for men and women.

Changes in sleep, weight, blood pressure, cholesterol and hormone levels often become more noticeable during this decade.

Many people remain healthy and active, but begin noticing that their body responds differently than it did ten years earlier.

What’s in this article

  • Common health changes people notice in their 40s
  • What those changes can mean
  • How menopause and perimenopause fit into the picture
  • Ways to build a clearer picture of your health

The 40s are often a decade where changes begin to add up. They may not stop you from doing the things you enjoy, but they can make you feel a little different to how you felt ten years ago.

Many people notice that seven or eight hours of sleep no longer feels as restorative as it once did. For women, changing hormone levels during perimenopause can contribute to disrupted sleep.

Stress, family responsibilities and changing sleep patterns can also make deep, uninterrupted sleep less common than it was in earlier decades.

A common observation during the 40s is that weight becomes easier to gain around the abdomen, even when eating habits have not changed significantly. This can occur because muscle mass naturally begins to decline with age, which affects how efficiently the body uses energy. Hormonal changes can also influence where fat is stored.

People often describe feeling more drained after a demanding week at work, several late nights, or a packed family schedule. Mental workload, stress and competing responsibilities can have a greater impact than they did in earlier years.

Walking into a room and forgetting why. Losing track of a conversation. Struggling to focus on several tasks at once.

While occasional forgetfulness is common at any age, hormonal changes, sleep disruption and stress can all affect concentration during the 40s.

Many people know their age, shoe size and coffee order. Far fewer know their blood pressure, cholesterol level or blood sugar results.

Blood pressure, cholesterol and blood sugar levels often become more relevant during this decade.

Most people cannot feel changes in these measurements, which is why they are sometimes discovered during routine health checks rather than because symptoms develop.

You can find more specific information about blood tests by Healthdirect Australiahere.

At Sunstate Family Practice & Skin Cancer Clinic, we recommend most people in their 40s get a blood test every 2-3 years, or when their doctor advises. Your medical history, family history, symptoms and lifestyle can all influence how often testing is recommended.

For women, the 40s can also be the decade when perimenopause begins.

Changes may include:

  • irregular periods
  • heavier or lighter bleeding
  • sleep disruption
  • hot flushes
  • difficulty concentrating
  • changes in mood

The timing and experience vary considerably between women, which is why individual assessment is important.

We go into more detail on the menopause journeyin this article.

One of the challenges of being in your 40s is that many changes happen gradually.

It can be difficult to tell whether something is simply part of getting older, related to menopause, or connected to another aspect of your health.

This is one reason the bulk-billed 45-49 Year Old Health Check exists. It provides an opportunity to review areas such as blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes risk, lifestyle factors and overall wellbeing in a structured way.

At Sunstate Family Practice, these conversations form part of a broader preventive health approach. The goal is to understand your current health, establish a baseline, and identify opportunities to stay well in the years ahead.
